# Rationale: P6.SerialParallel

## What the statement says
With `serial s p := ∏ i ∈ s, p i` and `parallel s q := 1 − ∏ i ∈ s, (1 − q i)` (real numbers,
`s : Finset ι`, `ι : Type`):

1. **Serial route.** If `0 ≤ p i ∧ p i ≤ 1` for all `i ∈ s`, then `serial s p ≤ p j` for every `j ∈ s`.
2. **Parallel routes.** If `0 ≤ q i ∧ q i ≤ 1` for all `i ∈ s`, then for every `j ∈ s`,
   `q j ≤ parallel s q` and `parallel s q ≤ 1`.
3. **Two routes, equality case.** For `0 ≤ p ≤ 1` and `0 ≤ q ≤ 1`:
   `1 − (1 − p) * (1 − q) = max p q ↔ (min p q = 0 ∨ max p q = 1)`.

## Hypotheses (complete list)
- Parts 1 and 2: `ι : Type`, `s : Finset ι`, a function `ι → ℝ`, and the bounds `[0, 1]` on `s`.
  No decidable equality on `ι` is assumed. A proof can use `classical` for `Finset.erase`.
- Part 3: `p q : ℝ` with `0 ≤ p ≤ 1`, `0 ≤ q ≤ 1`.

## Algebra of part 3 (checked by hand before proposing)
1 − (1 − p)(1 − q) = p + q − pq = p + q(1 − p) = q + p(1 − q).
- If p ≥ q, then max = p and min = q, and the left side equals p iff q(1 − p) = 0, that is iff q = 0 or p = 1,
  which is iff min = 0 or max = 1.
- If q ≥ p, the argument is symmetric: p(1 − q) = 0 iff p = 0 or q = 1.
When p = q both cases agree. As an extra check, I wrote a throwaway Lean proof of all three parts in a
scratch directory and it compiled. It is not part of this proposal and no proof is claimed.

## Why it is the right target
It is the reliability half of the corridor kernel. A route that chains links is no stronger than its
weakest link, and redundant alternative routes are no weaker than the best one. Part 3 says when
redundancy buys nothing: when the alternative is dead or the best route is already certain.
It completes the P6 ladder: MinimalNetwork (cost of connectivity), SingleFailureRobust (cost of redundancy),
SerialParallel (value of redundancy).

## Relation to P3.CascadedRecovery
Part 1 is the same inequality as part 3 of the sealed `P3.CascadedRecovery`, `∏ (1 − r i) ≤ 1 − r j`,
with `p i = 1 − r i`. It is restated here self-contained over `Finset` rather than `Fin n`, and nothing is
imported from P3.

## Independence (a field assumption)
`parallel s q` equals the probability that at least one route succeeds **only if the routes fail
independently**. Independence is a field assumption. It is not part of the mathematics, and a PROVEN
label for this statement would not certify it. Routes that share a corridor or a disturbance are not
independent, and for them the formula can overstate reliability. The statement is about the real-valued
formula only.

## Sanity checks in the module (by `norm_num`; `decide` does not apply to ℝ)
- Links 1/2 and 1/2 in series: `serial = 1/4 < 1/2`.
- Routes 1/2 and 1/2 in parallel: `parallel = 3/4 > 1/2`.
- Part 3 at p = q = 1/2: the left side 3/4 ≠ 1/2 = max, and neither min = 0 nor max = 1.
- Part 3 equality cases: (p, q) = (1/2, 0) and (1, 1/2).

## Negative control
Part 1 fails if some `p i > 1`. With links 2 and 1/2 the product is 1 > 1/2. The module checks this.

## What the statement does not settle
- Any real link or route probability, and the independence of routes.
- Equality cases for more than two routes, and strict inequalities, are not stated.
- Network reliability for general graphs (where routes share edges) is not covered.
