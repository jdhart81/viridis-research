# Rationale: P6.MinimalNetwork

## What the statement says
For every finite type of patches `V : Type` (with `Fintype V`, `DecidableEq V`) and every corridor
network `G : SimpleGraph V` with `DecidableRel G.Adj`, writing `n = patchCount V = Fintype.card V`
and `m = corridorCount G = G.edgeFinset.card`:

1. **Lower bound.** `G.Connected → n ≤ m + 1`.
2. **Trees are exactly the minimal connected networks.** `G.Connected ∧ m + 1 = n ↔ G.IsTree`.
3. **No spare corridor.** `G.IsTree → ∀ e ∈ G.edgeSet, ¬ (G.deleteEdges {e}).Connected`.

## Hypotheses (complete list)
- `V : Type` (universe 0), `[Fintype V]`, `[DecidableEq V]`.
- `G : SimpleGraph V`, `[DecidableRel G.Adj]` (needed so that `G.edgeFinset` exists).
- Part 1: `G.Connected`. Part 2: none beyond the above (it is an iff). Part 3: `G.IsTree` and `e ∈ G.edgeSet`.

The instance arguments are universally quantified, so the statement holds for any instances, not
just canonical ones. `patchCount` and `corridorCount` are transparent abbreviations defined inside
the namespace.

## Why it is the right target
It is the formal half of the HDFM claim "a dendritic network is the cheapest way to connect patches,
and for exactly that reason it has no spare corridor". Part 1 is the price of connectivity, part 2
says the price is met exactly by trees (dendritic networks), and part 3 says every corridor of such a
network is a bridge. It is the first rung of the P6 ladder; `P6.SingleFailureRobust` is the second
and reuses exactly these facts (restated, not imported).

## Mathlib hints checked against the pinned Mathlib (8f9d9cff)
All four exist under these names:
- `SimpleGraph.Connected.card_vert_le_card_edgeSet_add_one : G.Connected → Nat.card V ≤ Nat.card G.edgeSet + 1`
- `SimpleGraph.isTree_iff_connected_and_card [Finite V] : G.IsTree ↔ G.Connected ∧ Nat.card G.edgeSet + 1 = Nat.card V`
- `SimpleGraph.isAcyclic_iff_forall_edge_isBridge`
- `SimpleGraph.isBridge_iff : G.IsBridge s(u, v) ↔ G.Adj u v ∧ ¬(G \ fromEdgeSet {s(u, v)}).Reachable u v`
They are stated with `Nat.card`, so a proof has to convert to `Fintype.card` and `edgeFinset.card`
(`Nat.card_eq_fintype_card`, `SimpleGraph.edgeFinset_card`). Also present: `SimpleGraph.IsTree.card_edgeFinset`.

## Sanity checks in the module (all by `decide`)
- `path3` (edges 0–1, 1–2 on `Fin 3`) is connected.
- `corridorCount path3 = 2` and `corridorCount path3 + 1 = patchCount (Fin 3)`.
- `¬ (path3.deleteEdges {s(0, 1)}).Connected`.
- Negative control: `triangleAndIsolated` on `Fin 4` (edges 0–1, 1–2, 0–2; vertex 3 isolated) has
  `m + 1 = n` (3 + 1 = 4) and is not connected.

## Negative control
Part 2 is false without connectivity, i.e. `m + 1 = n → IsTree` fails: the triangle plus an isolated
vertex on `Fin 4` has `m + 1 = n` but is not a tree. It has a cycle and vertex 3 is isolated. The module
checks the count and the disconnection by `decide`. It is not a tree, because `IsTree` requires `Connected`.

## What the statement does not settle
- Nothing about geometry, corridor width, quality, cost or length: it counts edges only. Minimum
  *cost* networks (minimum spanning trees) are not covered.
- Nothing about whether a mapped corridor is used by any species.
- Infinite patch sets are not covered (`Fintype V`); universe-polymorphic `V` is not covered (`V : Type`).
- It does not discharge the FaceCount assumption of *Trees Cannot Cut* (project P1).
- It is classical graph theory, not a new theorem.

## Consistency check (not a claim)
To make sure the target is not false as written, I wrote a throwaway Lean proof of `Statement` in a
scratch directory outside `out/`, and it compiled under the pinned toolchain. That proof is not part of
this proposal, no proof is claimed, and the proving task stays open.
