A corridor network that is as cheap as possible (a tree) loses connectivity when any one corridor is lost. We give a Lean proof that, on two or more habitat patches, surviving any single corridor loss needs at least one corridor per patch, one more than a tree has. A triangle and a four-patch ring meet this bound exactly. Where the extra corridor goes matters as well: with exactly one extra, only a single loop through all patches survives every single loss. This is pure graph theory and makes no claim about real landscapes.
