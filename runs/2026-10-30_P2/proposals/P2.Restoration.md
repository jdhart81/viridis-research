## Kernel decision it backs

The Restoration kernel makes a go/no-go call on a planted cluster. A cluster below the critical
nucleus `n* = rStar^3` sits on the uphill side of the nucleation barrier and is expected to
dissolve (no-go). A cluster at or above `n*` is past the barrier top and is expected to grow (go).
The barrier height `ΔG* = ½·Δμ·n*` is the quantity the kernel reports as the cost of
establishment. The statement certifies only the mathematics of this rule: `rStar` is
well-defined and positive, it is the unique barrier top on `r ≥ 0`, and the barrier height
satisfies the half identity. The runtime identifier and TypeScript source of the Restoration
kernel were not among this session's inputs, so code conformance is not addressed here.

## What the statement says

`Statement` is the conjunction of two universally quantified parts. Each part keeps exactly its
canon theorem's hypotheses.

1. For all real `Δμ, a, σ` with `0 < Δμ`, `0 < a` and `0 < σ`:
   `0 < rStar`, `G r ≤ G rStar` for all `r ≥ 0`, and `G r = G rStar` with `r ≥ 0` implies
   `r = rStar`. This is `critical_nucleus` word for word.
2. For all real `Δμ, a, σ` with `0 < Δμ`: `G rStar = (1/2)·Δμ·rStar^3`. This is
   `barrier_half_identity` word for word. Like the canon, it carries no hypothesis on `a` or `σ`.

## Hypotheses (complete list)

| Hypothesis | Part | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `0 < Δμ` | 1, 2 | Driving force favours forest. Without it `rStar < 0` (control 6) and `G` is not bounded above on `r ≥ 0`. |
| `0 < a` | 1 | Shape factor. Needed for `rStar > 0`. |
| `0 < σ` | 1 | Surface tension (edge-mortality excess). Needed for `rStar > 0`. |
| `0 ≤ r` | 1 (inner) | Physical order parameter. Needed: control 4 shows maximality fails at `r = −1`. |

Observation, not part of the target: part 2 is a rational-function identity. In Lean, where
`x/0 = 0`, it would also hold at `Δμ = 0`. We keep `0 < Δμ` because the canon theorem and the
kernel's domain have it, and dropping it would strengthen the canon statement.

## Why this is the right target

- It restates the two theorems the brief names, with the canon definitions and hypotheses, and
  nothing more. In particular, `broadcast_suboptimal` (the dispersal-establishment paradox) is
  not included. It backs a different decision, about seed allocation, and could become its own
  statement.
- Combining both parts in one `Prop` follows the single-`Statement` module convention.
- It is not trivial. On this session's local probe run, none of 28 one-shot tactics (`rfl`,
  `decide`, `simp`, `norm_num`, `aesop`, `linarith`, `nlinarith`, `positivity`, `grind`,
  `field_simp`, `ring`, `polyrith`, and others) closed `Statement` after `unfold Statement`.

P2 order is Carbon Continuity (sealed, P2-T001), then Restoration, Afforestation and Harmonization.

## Sanity examples in the module (all `norm_num`; over ℝ, `decide` does not apply)

1. Witness `(Δμ, a, σ) = (1, 1, 3/2)`: `rStar = 1`, `G(1) = 1/2 = ½·1·1³`, so the barrier is positive.
2. Sub-critical: `G(1/2) = 1/4 < G(1)`.
3. Super-critical: `G(2) = −2 < G(1)`.
4. **Negative control:** `G(−1) = 5/2 > G(rStar)`, so the `r ≥ 0` restriction is essential.
5. **Negative control:** `G(rStar) ≠ Δμ·rStar³`, so the factor ½ is substantive.
6. **Negative control:** `Δμ = −1` gives `rStar < 0`, so part 1 needs `0 < Δμ`.
