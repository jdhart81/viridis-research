# Rationale: P3.LoopMassBalance

## What the statement says
One material circulates in discrete periods. Each period has demand `D`, a share `r` of it is met
from recovered material, and the rest, `V = D(1 - r)`, is virgin input. A share `rho` of the
secondary stock survives each period and recovered material `rD` flows into it, so
`S(t+1) = rho S(t) + r D`. The module defines `virgin`, `stock` (recursive over `ℕ`),
`steadyStock = rD/(1 - rho)` and `cumulativeVirgin D r T` (the sum of `virgin D r` over
`Finset.range T`). `Statement` asserts:

1. `0 ≤ V ≤ D`;
2. closed form `S(t) - S* = rho^t (S(0) - S*)` for all `t : ℕ`;
3. `S(t) → S*` (`Filter.Tendsto … atTop (𝓝 S*)`);
4. `cumulativeVirgin D r T = T·D·(1 - r)`;
5. for `D > 0`, `T > 0`: `r ↦ cumulativeVirgin D r T` is `StrictAntiOn` `[0, 1]`.

## Hypotheses, all listed
- Parts 1–4: `0 ≤ D`, `0 ≤ r`, `r ≤ 1`, `0 ≤ rho`, `rho < 1`; `S(0)` arbitrary real.
- Part 5: `0 < D`, `0 < T` (natural number); `r` ranges over `Set.Icc 0 1`.
- Model assumptions (not hypotheses in Lean, but built into the definitions): one material,
  constant demand, constant recovery and retention, linear stock update, no lag between
  recovery and reuse.

## Why it is the right target
It is the smallest closed mass-balance statement a recycling kernel needs. It gives a per-period
split into virgin and recovered input, a stable secondary stock with an explicit geometric rate,
and monotonicity of the cumulative virgin draw in the recovery fraction. Part 2 is stronger than
convergence alone: it gives the exact gap after `t` periods, which a kernel can use for
time-to-steady-state estimates. Part 4 is stated as a sum, not as a definition, so the
closed form is a theorem.

## Controls (examples in the module, all checked by `norm_num`)
- Witness `D = 10, r = 1/2, rho = 1/2, S(0) = 0`: `V = 5`, `S = 0, 5, 15/2, …`, `S* = 10`, and the
  gap at `t = 2` equals `(1/2)^2 (0 - 10)`.
- Cumulative virgin input `15` over 3 periods, which falls to `15/2` at `r = 3/4`.
- Negative control `rho = 1`: the stock grows without bound (`S(3) = 3`), Lean's `x/0 = 0`
  sets `S* = 0`, and the closed form fails, so `rho < 1` is necessary.
- Negative control `D = 0`: the cumulative virgin input does not depend on `r`, so strictness
  needs `D > 0`.
- Negative control `r = 2`: virgin input is negative, so `r ≤ 1` is necessary.

## Ladder
This is the first rung of P3. CascadedRecovery refines the single `r` into a product of stage
recoveries, so a cascade's effective recovery is `1 - ∏(1 - r_i)` and can be fed into `r` here.

## What the statement does not settle
It makes no claim about any real recovery rate, retention rate, demand level or material. Those
are field inputs. It does not model losses during recovery (quality degradation, downcycling),
time-varying demand, multiple materials, or stock limits on recovery (here `rD` is assumed
available whatever `S(t)` is). The secondary stock is bookkeeping and does not feed back into `r`.
