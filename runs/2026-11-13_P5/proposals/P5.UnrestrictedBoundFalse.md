# Rationale: `P5.UnrestrictedBoundFalse`

## What the statement says

The module restates over Mathlib, inside its own namespace, the two historical definitions of
`Gaia_Intelligence_Theorem.lean` (January 2026, SHA-256 `37c017145dcf…2098c4`):

    landauerEnergy (kB T : ℝ) : ℝ := kB * T * Real.log 2
    maxBitRate (P kB T : ℝ) : ℝ := P / landauerEnergy kB T

and states

    ¬ (∀ dIdt P D kB T : ℝ,
        kB > 0 → T > 0 → P ≥ 0 → D ≥ 0 → D ≤ 1 →
        dIdt ≤ maxBitRate P kB T * D)

that is: the historical axiom `intelligence_bound`, read literally, is false. The guards in the
statement are copied from the axiom's text in the same order. Nothing is imported or copied from
the historical module, and no axiom is declared.

**This result is elementary.** A proof instantiates the universal statement at the witness
`dIdt = 1, P = 0, D = 0, kB = T = 1` (all guards hold) and obtains `1 ≤ 0`. The witness is
deliberately kept out of the statement so that the proof task (P5-T002) still has content.

## Why this is the right target

The finding concerns the exact formal text, and the exact consequence of that text is that a
Lean environment assuming it is inconsistent. Stating the negation of the axiom's body, with the
definitions it depends on restated verbatim, is the smallest statement that captures that and
nothing else. I considered stating "assuming the axiom proves `False`", but that is the same
proposition (`¬ A` unfolds to `A → False`) and the negation reads more directly.

## Relation to the ladder

P5 has two rungs. This statement (P5-T002) is the first: it settles Finding 1. The second,
`P5.CompensationUnderCeiling` (P5-T003), settles Finding 2 and states what does hold about
compensation. The two are independent; neither imports the other.

## Controls (sanity checks in the module)

- `maxBitRate 0 1 1 = 0` (by `simp`), and `landauerEnergy 1 1 = ln 2 > 0`.
- The witness meets every guard and violates the conclusion (by `norm_num` over ℝ).
- The guards and the arithmetic core `¬ (1 ≤ 0 · 0)` checked by `decide` on ℕ. Real-number
  equalities involving `Real.log` are not decidable, so the checks over ℝ use `simp`/`norm_num`,
  as the sealed modules of other projects do; the `decide` check covers the integer skeleton.
- **Negative control:** adding the hypothesis `dIdt ≤ maxBitRate P kB T * D` makes the
  implication trivially true (proved in the module by `fun … h => h`). This shows that the
  missing ingredient is a condition tying the rate `dIdt` to a system; without one the rate is an
  arbitrary real number.
- A second control: any rate `dIdt ≤ 0` satisfies the bound when `P, D ≥ 0` and `kB, T > 0`
  (proved in the module), so every counterexample needs a positive rate above the capacity.

## What the statement does not establish

- Nothing about the Intelligence Bound as a physical conjecture. A physical version would bound
  the rate of a modelled system, with the rate defined from that system; this statement only
  shows that the unrestricted text is false.
- Nothing about whether any agent preserves biodiversity.
- Nothing about other files of the corpus: per the project README, no declaration uses the
  axiom and no other file imports it. That is a claim about the corpus, read by a person, not
  something this statement checks.
